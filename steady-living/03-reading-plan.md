# Reading Plan

Four weeks per school, one short passage per week, each paired with the round prompt. Send the passage and prompt **a day ahead** (people can read it in 30 seconds and the round gets better).

> References are to classic, public-domain texts. Check exact wording in the edition you use. Good free translations: George Long (Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus), Cyril Bailey or R. D. Hicks (Epicurus), Acharya Buddharakkhita (Dhammapada), Thanissaro Bhikkhu (suttas).

## Month 1 — Stoicism (control, judgment, duty)

| Week | Passage | Gist | Round prompt |
|------|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1 | Some things are up to us, some aren't. | Where did I spend effort this week on something that wasn't mine to control? |
| 2 | Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.47 | It isn't the thing that disturbs you but your judgment of it, and you can wipe the judgment out. | What impression did I agree to without checking? |
| 3 | *Meditations* 5.1 | Rise at dawn to do the work of a human being, not to stay warm in bed. | Where did comfort win this week, and where didn't it? |
| 4 | *Meditations* 2.1 | You'll meet difficult people today; we were made to work together. | Who was hard to deal with, and what was my part? |

## Month 2 — Epicureanism (desire, pleasure, friendship)

Pairs well after Stoicism: the two schools disagree usefully about pleasure.

| Week | Passage | Gist | Round prompt |
|------|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus* — the three kinds of desire | Natural and necessary; natural but unnecessary; vain. | Which desire ran my week, and what kind was it? |
| 2 | *Letter to Menoeceus* — on weighing pleasures | Not every pleasure should be chosen; judge by what follows. | What did I choose that felt good then and cost me later? |
| 3 | *Principal Doctrines* 27 | Of all the things that bring a good life, friendship is the greatest. | Who did I really show up for this week? |
| 4 | *Vatican Sayings* 33 | The body's cry is only: not to be hungry, thirsty or cold. | What did I think I needed this week that I could have done without? |

## Month 3 — Buddhist thought, secular (craving, attention, the pause)

Keep it secular: the psychology and the practice, not the religion, so the "no creed" promise holds.

| Week | Passage | Gist | Round prompt |
|------|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | *Dhammapada* 1–2 | The mind is the forerunner of all states. | How did my mood shape what I saw this week? |
| 2 | The "second arrow" (*Sallatha Sutta*, SN 36.6) | Pain is the first arrow; our reaction adds the second. | Where did I add a second arrow? |
| 3 | *Dhammapada* 103 | Conquering yourself is greater than conquering a thousand in battle. | Where did I win or lose against myself? |
| 4 | Silent practice week (no text) | Spend most of the hour watching an urge rise and pass. | What did I notice about wanting? |

## Month 4 (optional) — Aristotle (habit and character)

Start with *Nicomachean Ethics* II.1: we become just by doing just acts; character comes from repeated action. Good if the group asks "how do habits shape who we are?" Later options: the doctrine of the mean (*NE* II.6), friendship (*NE* VIII).

## Later candidates

- **Confucian thought** — roles and relationships (*Analects*).
- **Repeat a school** the group found most useful, with fresh passages.

## Using the plan

- A conversation that runs long is worth more than finishing the passage. Skip or repeat a week freely.
- If the passage grows past five minutes, trim it.
- At the end of each month ask: *which school helped most?* That decides what comes next.
