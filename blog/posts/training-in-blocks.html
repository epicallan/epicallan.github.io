Training in blocks: Block 1 recap and Block 2 plan

Why blocks🔗

I train three days a week at a club before work, with swimming on the other days, and a hard one-hour cap on each session. Rather than one open-ended plan, I run the program in blocks of a few weeks. Each block has a fixed structure, the lifts progress inside it, and a Sunday review at the end decides what changes for the next one.

The live version, with exercise demos and the numbers I need to beat next session, is on my training page.

Block 1 (8 weeks, 09-17 → 11-15)🔗

It started as a 5-day split and became a 3-day split two weeks in: A Push, B Pull, C Legs-arms-shoulders, rotating across Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Gym and swim on the same day were taking close to two hours, so each day now gets one activity.

  • From week 3: a one-hour cap and rest on a timer (2 min between sets, 3 min between exercises, supersets as pairs).
  • Late October: a Saturday volume session (bench, squat, deadlift) and an optional Sunday light pull.
  • The squat was reset with a coach to an empty-ish bar to fix form, then rebuilt.

Block 2 (4 weeks, 11-16 → 12-13)🔗

The week is fixed: Monday C, Wednesday A, Friday B, plus the weekend sessions. Each weekday is one main lift plus three extras, sized to fit inside the hour.

  • Squat: heavy on Monday (3×5, starting at 40kg, +5kg whenever all 15 reps are clean), volume on Saturday.
  • Arms: two short supersets added, about four minutes a day, with no extra rest.
  • Deload: at the end of the block, or sooner if two sessions in a row stall.

Rules that carry over🔗

  • Progressive overload: main lifts take the smallest jump once every prescribed rep is clean; extras stay at a weight until every set reaches the top of the range.
  • Effort: extras can go to failure on the last set; the big lifts stop at the last rep with good form.
  • Missed days: a missed weekday's exercises move to the weekend, and the rotation carries on. Never miss twice.
  • The cap: if a session runs long, drop the last superset rather than rushing the main lift.

This is my own training log, not medical advice.
